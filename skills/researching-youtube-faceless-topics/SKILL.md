# Researching YouTube Faceless Topics

## Overview

Build a defensible shortlist of specific YouTube Faceless niches from current search and platform signals. Separate verified observations from judgment, narrow crowded topics into viable long-tail angles, and deliver the exact schema requested.

**Required companion skill:** Use `Spreadsheets` whenever the deliverable is `.xlsx`.

## Workflow

1. **Lock the brief.** Confirm target country, research window, topic count, audience language, output columns, and filename. If the window includes an incomplete month, state the data cutoff.
2. **Build seed clusters.** Cover seasonal intent, evergreen problems, buying intent, hobbies, education, practical tools, and timely events. Avoid treating “AI” as the subject unless requested; AI may be the production method.
3. **Collect public signals.** Prefer Google Trends with the correct country, dates, and YouTube Search property. Add YouTube autocomplete, recent search results, creator/video traction, and authoritative calendars or reports. Record source, date, market, query, and property.
4. **Validate demand.** Require at least two independent signals for each finalist when feasible. Label unavailable, proxy, partial-period, or inferred evidence. Never invent search volume, trend scores, or growth percentages.
5. **Niche down.** Replace broad or saturated ideas with a concrete audience, problem, format, season, product category, or long-tail query.
6. **Apply hard gates.** Reject weak demand, high copyright dependence, unsafe policy exposure, low originality, poor AI producibility, or topics that cannot support both long-form and Shorts.
7. **Score and rank internally.** Evaluate demand, growth/timing, competition opportunity, AI production fit, monetization, and policy/copyright safety. Use the scoring model in [references/workflow.md](references/workflow.md); do not expose invented precision.
8. **Write final rows.** One specific topic and one best primary keyword per row. Use only the audience language, remove semantic duplicates, and rank strongest first.
9. **QA and export.** Validate row count, numbering, exact headers, uniqueness, language, filename, links, and workbook rendering.

## Output Contract

- Obey the user's exact column count and order.
- Keep the topic distinct from the primary keyword; do not add supporting keywords to a single-keyword field.
- Do not put scores or source notes into the workbook unless requested.
- Provide the downloadable file plus a concise methodology note and limitations outside the sheet.
- Preserve original analysis: each topic must support substantive scripts, commentary, and editing rather than reused or mass-produced filler.

## Fast Decisions

| Situation | Action |
| --- | --- |
| Exact Trends property data is accessible | Use it and record the configuration |
| Only public proxy evidence is accessible | Use cautiously and disclose the limitation |
| High demand, high competition | Find a narrower long-tail angle |
| Strong traffic, heavy copyrighted footage | Reject or redesign around original visuals |
| Current month is incomplete | Mark the as-of date; do not compare as a full month |

## Common Mistakes

- Presenting estimates as measured search volume.
- Mixing languages in one market list.
- Ranking by virality alone while ignoring monetization and policy risk.
- Keeping broad labels such as “finance,” “fitness,” or “travel.”
- Treating an attractive spreadsheet as proof that the research is sound.

## Example

For “United States, April–September 2026, 50 rows, English, exactly `STT | Chủ đề | Keyword`,” research in English, disclose the September cutoff if partial, rank 50 unique niches, and export only those three columns.
